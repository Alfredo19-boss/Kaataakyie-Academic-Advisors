What actually goes wrong

Five traps that sink finished applications

None of these is about how good the applicant is.

The funding deadline is the real deadline

Schools publish a final deadline and, weeks earlier, a priority or funding deadline. Apply to the later one and the assistantships are gone before the file is read. Aim at the earlier date, every time.

Section minimums, not just the total

A comfortable overall English score with one weak section is still a rejection. Many programmes set a floor on speaking or writing separately, and it is the most common avoidable failure in the whole process.

The credential evaluation takes months

Six to twelve weeks from request to delivered report, longer when the issuing registrar is slow to verify. Started in month two it is invisible; started in month nine it decides the season.

Letters go missing, quietly

Recommenders upload through each school's own portal, separately. Nobody tells the applicant one is outstanding. Chase at two weeks out, not two days, and track them by name and by school.

Interview slots, not documents

Once the I-20 is in hand the bottleneck is the embassy calendar, which is longest in the months before a Fall intake. Book the moment the DS-160 is filed rather than waiting for everything else to be ready.
